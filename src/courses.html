Ethnic Studies Courses

The Department of Ethnic Studies examines the historical, cultural, political, and economic experiences of racialized and Indigenous peoples across Hawaiʻi, Oceania, Asia, and the United States. The undergraduate curriculum analyzes systems of colonization, immigration, race, class, and gender while emphasizing community engagement, social justice, and diverse cultural perspectives.

Lower Division (100–200 Level)

Introductory coursework establishes foundational concepts and theories for analyzing ethnic group experiences in relation to colonization, racism, social class, and migration. Students explore global race and ethnic relations, critical media analysis in popular culture, Native Hawaiian social systems and land stewardship, and the cultural production and identity formation of the Filipino diaspora.

{% endcall %}
{% call imageTextRow('/media/units/es/courses/course-02.webp', 600, 600, 'Participants listening during a group discussion') %}

Upper Division (300–400 Level)

Upper-division courses offer intensive examinations of specific communities, historical movements, and intersectional frameworks across Hawaiʻi, the Pacific, and the continental United States. Topics include the histories and contemporary issues of Native Hawaiians, Asian Americans, Pacific Islanders, African Americans, American Indians, and Latinx communities, alongside specialized studies in land tenure, labor history, law, environmental justice, gender and sexuality, oral history, and qualitative research methods. Students also apply classroom theory through community fieldwork, service learning, and documentary film production.

{% endcall %}
