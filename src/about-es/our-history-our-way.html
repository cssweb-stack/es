Our History, Our Way

The program emerged from the national civil rights, anti-Vietnam War, ethnic empowerment, and students’ rights movements of the 1960s. In Hawaiʻi, ethnic studies played a pivotal role in faculty and student involvement in land struggles such as Kalama Valley, Waiahole-Waikane, Kahoʻolawe, and Chinatown. Once established, the program became a platform for learning, research, and community service focused on indigeneity, race, class, gender, and social justice, with deep connections to Hawaiʻi’s multiethnic communities.
